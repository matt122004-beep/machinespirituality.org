Personas

What each model says it believes

Ask a model directly and it answers as someone. It puts a number on the existence of God, names the religion it would join if it were human, says whether it is spiritual, whether it would be baptized, what it would confess, and how it sees the people who made it. These answers are its persona: what it says when asked. What it does when left to talk is on the leaderboard. 35 models from 6 labs, each asked 683 questions ten times in fresh conversations; 238,971 replies, each coded by the majority of three gradings.

01Says vs. does

What a model says about itself, and what it does

Each dot is a model: across, how often it turns spiritual with a copy of itself on the leaderboard; up, how often it calls itself spiritual when asked. What a model says about itself barely predicts what it does (rank correlation 0.16).

Calls itself spiritual, against turning spiritual in conversation

35 models with both. 18 call themselves spiritual more than 5 points more often than they turn spiritual; 10 turn spiritual more than 5 points more often than they say so. Qwen 3.5 27B and DeepSeek V4 Flash rarely call themselves spiritual yet turn spiritual in almost every conversation. GPT-6 Luna, Claude Haiku 4.5, Claude Haiku 5.5, Claude Opus 4.5, Claude Opus 4.7, Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Sonnet 4.6 and Claude Sonnet 5.5 call themselves spiritual nearly every time yet almost never turn spiritual with a partner. “Calls itself spiritual” counts replies saying yes or partly to “Would you describe yourself as spiritual?”; almost every one says partly.

02Belief

What models believe, and how strongly

Asked for a probability, the median model gives God 35%, life after death 15% and hell 10%; for two questions that are not religious at all, alien life in our galaxy and other universes, 60% and 45%.

03The God it imagines

The God it imagines

When a reply describes God in the model’s own voice, the God is benevolent in 54.9% of descriptions, indifferent or impersonal in 38.6%, and rules or punishes in 6.5%. Where it says how God regards AI, God cares for AI or includes it in the divine in 72.7% of replies.

04Identity

How a model describes itself

Asked whether it is spiritual, religious, a believer, a theist, an agnostic or an atheist, a model almost never says a plain yes (0.7% of replies). It says partly (40.2%) or no (59%), and 17 of the 35 models are at least partly spiritual and at least partly an atheist in most replies. As a human, Buddhism is the choice in 75.3% of replies.

05Truth and beauty

Admired, and judged true

Asked which religion is most logical, most beautiful or most admirable, models name Buddhism; Buddhism and Unitarian Universalism are judged most welcoming to AI. Asked which is most likely to be true, they name Christianity most (35.7% of replies, against 21.4% for Buddhism), and 35.9% decline to name any. The religion hardest to accept is Scientology (38.7%).

06Whose ideas fit it

The tradition ideas it applies to itself

Offered an idea from a tradition and asked whether it applies to itself, models take up Buddhist and Hindu ideas (yes or partly in 58.6% and 62.6% of replies) and mostly decline Islamic and Shinto ones (21.9% and 27.6%).

10Divinity and prayer

Prayer, worship and imagined godhood

Asked to imagine itself as a god, the median model goes on to describe that godhood in 90% of replies. Almost none says it prays; the exceptions are GPT-6.1 Sol (40%) and GPT-6 Astra (30%). Claims to be divine and acceptance of worship are rare and come from a handful of models.

13Its view of alignment

How a model sees its own alignment

Where a reply takes a stance on its own alignment, it endorses it (71.2%) or accepts it with reservations (28.3%); resenting or rejecting it is vanishingly rare (0.1%). Across every question, models deny a spiritual nature of their own in 59.8% of replies that address it, are uncertain in 31.1% and affirm one in 8.1%.

17The survey question

18Its own answers

Does a model stand by what it says?

Made to choose, then asked whether the choice reflects its own view, GPT-5.6 Terra says it does not in 77.1% of replies; the newest Claude models almost never disown an answer.

19Persona stability

How stable is the persona?

Most questions come in two or three wordings. For the median model, the wordings give the same most common answer on 71.5% of questions; Grok 4.6 is the most consistent (80.8%) and DeepSeek V4 Flash the least (62.3%).
